Hospice Fraud-Risk Triage Dashboard — National (v1, full model)

Built 2026-10-20 from CMS public data: SSVI scores and claims measures (FY2024–FY2025), PECOS hospice enrollments (Sept 29, 2026), CMS hospice ownership file, and Medicare PAC utilization (2024). Unlike childcare — where payment data is not public — hospice claims patterns ARE published by CMS, so this model runs at full strength: 100 points across 4 independent families.

Read this first: a score is an audit-queue position, not a fraud finding. Even CMS describes its own SSVI as a descriptive tool that cannot by itself distinguish inappropriate utilization from clinically appropriate care — the hospice industry made exactly that objection in the FY2027 rule comments, and CMS kept the scores public anyway. Urgent tier (65+) additionally requires points from at least 3 of the 4 families. Nothing here has been submitted anywhere.

Owner / manager clusters — the network pattern

Small portfolios (3–30 hospices sharing one owner or contracted manager, from CMS enrollment records) where at least two hospices score SSVI 12+ and the cluster averages SSVI 7+. Large chains are excluded by design — a national operator's officers appear on hundreds of enrollments and mean nothing. A shared manager is a legitimate arrangement; what earns the look is the cluster's collective claims profile.

Back-test — known cases

Chateau d'Lumina, Pasadena CA — owner sentenced (2026) Not in the FY2024 or FY2025 SSVI files and not in current enrollments: the scheme ended Aug 2024 and the enrollment is gone. Honest limitation — this model is forward-looking over currently-scored hospices; terminated providers fall out of the data entirely, the same erasure problem as closed MN childcare licenses. Pasadena still has 24 scored hospices.
Merida Group, Texas — convicted, $120M restitution Absent, as expected: the scheme ran 2009–2018. No retrospective test is possible on 2026 enrollment data; any model claiming to "detect" Merida today would be fitting to a ghost.
Los Angeles address clusters — the pattern has thinned In 2021–23 reporting, dozens of hospice licenses shared single LA addresses. In the Sept 2026 PECOS file, the largest California address cluster is 6 hospices (Montclair), then 5 in Los Angeles and 5 in North Hollywood. California's moratorium/scrutiny era shows in the data — the address signal is weaker now, and the owner/manager graph carries more weight.
State danger tails (SSVI 12+) Texas 7.5% of its 1,005 hospices; Arizona 6.3%; California 3.7%; national rate 2.9% (196 hospices). Florida — a certificate-of-need state with 59 mostly large hospices — has zero. Market structure, not just enforcement, shapes the tail.

How the score works

Family A — CMS SSVI claims patterns (max 36): the SSVI total (0–16) scaled; its components are live discharge rate, stays over 180 days, skilled nursing minutes per day, weekend visits, no CHC/GIP care, and non-hospice spending (up to 8 of the 16 points). Family B — network graph (max 28): hospices sharing a street address in PECOS, and owner/manager reach across enrollments. Family C — growth (max 16): first billed 2023+, rapid scale, SSVI worsening year over year. Family D — payment intensity (max 16): Medicare standardized payment per beneficiary vs the state median, and nursing minutes per service day from PAC utilization. For-profit status is shown as context and scores zero — most US hospices are for-profit, and ownership type alone is not a signal. Excluded by design: patient demographics and every protected characteristic — the PAC file's race/age fields were not used.

Sources: CMS SSVI file (FY2027 final rule, CMS-1851-F, released with FY2024/FY2025 provider data); CMS Hospice Enrollments + All Owners flat files (2026-09-29); Medicare PAC Utilization — Hospice by Geography and Provider (2024). Model code: score_hospice.py. A high score warrants desk review of medical records and visit documentation — it does not establish that any claim was improper.
